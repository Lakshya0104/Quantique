01 · The problem

After a disaster, everyone needs to talk.
That's exactly when the network disappears.

Pain 1

Trapped, with a phone that can't call

Under rubble or stranded by water. The phone works, but there's no network to reach anyone.

Pain 2

Rescuers search blind

Nobody knows where people are, how many there are, or who is most critical.

Pain 3

Emergency networks jam

If every relay rebroadcasts every message, a small radio channel chokes exactly when traffic peaks.

We need a way to send SOS messages with zero infrastructure, using the phones people already have, that tells the sender the message arrived and stays fast when hundreds of people use it at once.

02 · Two situations, one network

Who is the survivor, and who brings the network?

SCENARIO A

Trapped, but has a phone

Under rubble, on a rooftop, cut off by flood. Can't move.

Rescuers bring the network to them:

  • carry nodes into the area
  • place relays where quantum planning says
  • detect phones, open "SOS" Wi-Fi
  • see everything on an offline dashboard
SCENARIO B

Can move, may have no phone

Mobile but cut off: a lost or dead phone, or no network.

They go to a public SOS point:

  • nodes pre-placed at hospitals, panchayats, stations
  • alert + map tell people where
  • use own phone, or the shared phone/tablet kept there
  • same relays, same dashboard

Both use the same node (ESP32 + LoRa), the same relay network and the same dashboard. Only who places the node changes.

05 · What the survivor sees · both scenarios

No app. Connect to Wi-Fi, and the SOS page opens.

Quick buttons first

Most urgent options at the top. One tap + 2 questions. Answers fit in a ~21-byte message.

Yes / No / Maybe

Short questions anyone can answer under stress. Local languages supported.

Custom text

Optional short note (≤ 40 characters) to the SOS station or to family at another node.

Location

Node's saved coordinates or its GPS, clearly labelled. Phone GPS isn't relied on (browsers block it on plain local pages).

Two status lights · on the page and on the node

● Orange: Sent. The node transmitted it. The page then shows "Delivered ✓" when the station's automatic receipt comes back.

● Green: Read by SOS. An operator opened it and assigned help. This only happens after a person acts, never automatically.

06 · The network · our core engineering

Fast for urgent cases, never jammed

1 · Forget repeats

Unique message ID; relays drop copies.

2 · Hop limit + expiry

Max 4 hops; old messages expire.

3 · Closer-only

Forward only if nearer the station. No blind flooding.

4 · Priority lanes

Rescue/medical first; waiting ones move up.

5 · Listen, then talk

Check the channel, short random wait, cancel if already sent.

6 · Capped retries

Retry with growing gaps, then stop.

≈ 0.3 s
per hop, urgent (est.)
≈ 1.6 s
3 hops + receipt (est.)
1 channel
more relays ≠ more capacity

Estimates, to be measured on our prototype. Collisions are reduced, not eliminated.

09 · Proof of concept · our kit

1 ESP32 · 2 LoRa radios · 2 antennas · breadboard

Demo script · 3 minutes
  1. Judge joins Wi-Fi "SOS-Rescue"; the SOS page opens
  2. Taps "Rescue now"; LoRa A sends it over the air; orange
  3. LoRa B receives it; a pin appears on the dashboard
  4. We click "Mark read"; the reply comes back; green
  5. Resend the same ID: dropped. Urgent jumps the queue.

Honest scope: one ESP32 runs both radios (shared SPI, separate CS pins), so this proves the message, receipt, status lights, priority and duplicate logic on a real radio link. Real relay hops need 1 ESP32 + 1 LoRa per node (≥ 3 boards): our next step.

Pins: SPI 18/19/23 shared · A: CS 5, RST 14, DIO0 26 · B: CS 4, RST 27, DIO0 25 · 3.3 V only · antennas on before TX

10 · Quantum · where it really helps

Quantum plans where rescuers put relays (Scenario A)

The problem: a team carries only a few relays. Put them in the wrong place and survivors stay cut off. Choosing K sites out of N is an optimisation that grows explosively with map size.

Our approach: turn the phone heatmap into survivor clusters → write it as a QUBO → solve with QAOA on Qiskit → show the chosen sites on the dashboard.

Our run (Qiskit Aer)Result
6 sites, place 2 relays6 qubits, p = 2
QAOA's choicesites 2 + 3 → 13 of 21 people
Exact answersame ✓

At this size a laptop is just as fast; we show the method works, not a speed-up. The radio network itself is fully classical.
