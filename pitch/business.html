ProblemAfter earthquakes, floods and cyclones, power and cellular fail first. Survivors still have phones but no network. Government alerts are one-way, and existing mesh radios need a device per person set up in advance.
SolutionA rescue kit: rescuer nodes (ESP32 + LoRa) that open a Wi-Fi SOS page for any phone and count nearby phones for a search heatmap; AI compression; quantum-planned, self-healing relays; and an offline command dashboard with Read/Dispatch status back to the survivor.
CustomerGovernment responders: NDRF, 16 SDRFs, district disaster management authorities, fire services. Also large-event organisers, mines, coastal fisheries and defence units.
ModelKit sales through government procurement (GeM), an annual command-software licence, installation and training contracts, and event rentals.

All prices are estimates from typical Indian retail listings for single units (2026). Volume pricing is lower. Validate with supplier quotes before any bid.

Risks and how we handle them

RiskMitigation
Phones with Wi-Fi off / dead battery can't be found or sendPush-button SOS on the node; works alongside dogs, radar and thermal search
Spoofed SOS / eavesdroppingPer-deployment keys + packet authentication (roadmap); no personal data collected
Radio regulationMove to 865–867 MHz with duty-cycle limits; one-line firmware change
AI misreads a messageTaps always win; AI can only raise severity; survivor's own words always shown
Procurement cycles are slowStart with CSR-funded shelter pilots and event rentals for early revenue
